# Out-research the trackers, don't out-track them

AISEO's real competitors in October 2026 are not the venture-funded enterprise platforms but the crowded field under $100 a month: self-serve trackers such as Otterly and Promptmonitor at $29, LLMrefs at $79, Peec at $95 and Trakkr at $100; incumbent suites that now bundle AI tracking cheaply or free, from HubSpot AEO at $50 and Yoast at about $30 to Wix, Ahrefs, SE Ranking and Semrush's $99 toolkit; free graders from Semrush, Ahrefs and HubSpot that need no signup; and a public tutorial app whose report design matches AISEO's down to its plan quotas. Above that field, capital has gathered around Profound ($180M at a $1.8B valuation in September 2026), and incumbents have bought their way in as Adobe took Semrush, Sitecore took Scrunch, HubSpot took XFunnel and Tracksuit took Hall. Nearly all of these vendors sell repeated monitoring of a fixed set of prompts, usually scraped from consumer AI interfaces just as AISEO's answers are, and that monitoring is now a commodity: $29 buys about **1,800 recurring answers a month at Otterly against 550 one-off answers at AISEO Pro**, and AISEO's own cost per answer (about $0.009–$0.02, more than 80% of it Claude) already equals what Peec, Otterly and Trakkr charge their customers, so a tracking price war is unwinnable. What AISEO can own is breadth-first research with visible evidence: for the same $29 it answers **425 distinct prompts against Otterly's 15**, in 20 markets, with analysis tied to the actual answers and citations, which trackers don't attempt and graders hide. To make that position credible, AISEO should replace single-run scan verdicts with repeated samples and ranges (about $0.03 more per scan), add a Google surface to keyword reports now that Perplexity's share of AI referrals has halved, cut Claude's share of each report's cost, meet the free graders with a no-signup preview, and charge agencies for white-label audits instead of calling a $79 plan with no agency features the agency tier.

### Incumbents are buying in while Profound moves up-market

Incumbents have chosen to buy rather than build. HubSpot launched a $50-a-month AEO product in April 2026, six months after agreeing to buy XFunnel ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/hubspot-launches-aeo-tool-as-organic-traffic-drops-27-for-its-customers/)). Adobe combined Semrush's AI data with its own LLM Optimizer into an enterprise product, Adobe Brand Visibility ([Adobe newsroom](https://news.adobe.com/news/2026/06/introducing-adobe-brand-visibility)). Sitecore plans to build Scrunch into its digital-experience platform ([Sitecore](https://www.sitecore.com/company/newsroom/press-releases/2026/06/sitecore-acquires-scrunch-to-help-brands-influence-discovery--and-buying-decisions)), and Hall's site now reads "Hall is now part of Tracksuit" ([Hall](https://usehall.com/)). The category has also been formally recognized: Gartner published its first Market Guide for Answer Engine Visibility Tools in March 2026, and G2's AEO category passed 150 products ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/7559273); [G2](https://company.g2.com/news/g2s-2025-year-in-review)). Because the buyers are marketing suites, experience platforms and a brand tracker, standalone monitoring is heading into suites as a feature.

The leader is moving up-market from the other side. **Profound dropped its $99 Starter and $399 Growth self-serve plans in mid-September 2026** and now lists only a free trial and custom Enterprise pricing, while its Series D pays for marketing agents, an AI-ads studio and models post-trained for marketing ([PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/tryprofound/diffs/2026W39); [Profound pricing](https://www.tryprofound.com/pricing)). Some September roundups still list the old plans, but the live page and PricingSaaS's change log agree they are gone. The buyer with less than $100 a month to spend has been left to small tools and to bundles.

### Buyers are paying for influence, not traffic

The money is not following referral traffic. AI chatbots sent **0.25% of tracked worldwide referrals in August 2026**, against 0.23% a year earlier, according to Statcounter data compiled by upgrowth ([upgrowth](https://upgrowth.in/ai-traffic-share-report-2026/)). Buyers are paying to protect their influence in answers that send no clicks, and three correlational studies point the same way. Ahrefs' 2026 re-run associated Google AI Overviews with a **58% lower click-through rate at position 1** ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260518322756/en/)); HubSpot says its customers' organic traffic fell 27% year over year ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/hubspot-launches-aeo-tool-as-organic-traffic-drops-27-for-its-customers/)); and Seer found that brands cited inside an AI Overview earned a 0.95% organic click-through rate, against 0.55% for brands left out ([Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update)). AISEO should therefore sell influence ("are you named and cited where buyers ask?") rather than AI traffic. It should also keep analyst forecasts out of the pitch: they put the market at about $1.1B in 2026 rising to $16–17B by 2034, but they are paywalled and internally inconsistent ([Superlines](https://www.superlines.io/articles/geo-market-size-statistics)).

The mix of engines matters more for product decisions. Between August 2025 and August 2026, **Gemini's share of AI chatbot referrals rose from 2.2% to 10.9%, while Perplexity's fell from 8.1% to 4.3%** ([upgrowth](https://upgrowth.in/ai-traffic-share-report-2026/)). Similarweb's August 2026 update puts Gemini at 25.4% of visits to generative-AI sites, up from 12.9%, with ChatGPT down from 73.3% to 56.7% ([Similarweb](https://x.com/Similarweb/status/2096878021378466096)). AISEO's keyword reports rely on Perplexity alone, an engine whose share of referrals has halved in a year.

## Four groups compete, but only the cheap ones contest AISEO's buyer

AISEO's competitors fall into four groups that work differently and threaten it differently. Ranked by how directly they compete for AISEO's buyer, the most dangerous are the incumbent bundles and free graders that give away the basic "am I mentioned?" check. Next come the self-serve trackers at $29–$100, then the prompt-research modules and tutorial apps that replicate the keyword report. Enterprise platforms come a distant fourth: they matter for the standard of measurement they set, not for their prices. The profiles below follow the market's own grouping. Prices are monthly list prices read from vendor pages on 13 October 2026 unless noted.

Collection methods differ, and vendors disclose them unevenly. Profound advertises "front-end browser querying that mirrors what real users see," run daily in 150+ regions. YC-backed Gauge sells a similar bundle at $599 a month for 600 daily prompts, according to an aggregator ([Stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/en/gauge)).

Independent reviews are scarce, and most comparison content is written by rival vendors. G2 reviewers praise Profound's depth and support but call it one of the most expensive tools, with a steep learning curve, data-heavy dashboards and API access limited to Enterprise ([G2](https://g2.com/products/profound/reviews?page=5)). Competitor-written reviews call Scrunch the most premium of its peers, and better at showing problems than at fixing them ([geotoolbox](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/scrunch-ai-review)). These vendors are not AISEO's price rivals, but they matter in two ways. They define what credible measurement looks like, with repeated runs and published margins of error. And their weak points (price, learning curve, dense dashboards) are exactly the complaints a cheap product that delivers reports can answer.

Where these vendors disclose a method, it is scraping the consumer interface. Trakkr, Geoptie, LLMrefs, Knowatoa and Am I On AI say nothing about method, and none of them names its data provider.

The core metrics (mentions, competitor share of voice, cited domains and URLs, sentiment) appear on nearly every plan, so vendors now compete on three other things: prompt discovery (Peec's 1–5 demand score, LLMrefs' expansion of each keyword into related prompts), action features (articles, site audits, Reddit and outreach lists) and agency packaging (white-label reports, pitch workspaces, Looker Studio, API and MCP).

Reviews praise ease of use and support: Otterly scores 4.7 on G2, with 9.3 for ease of use ([SE Visible review](https://visible.seranking.com/blog/otterly-ai-review/)). Practitioners call Knowatoa a "diagnostic grader" because "most stop at a number" ([IndustryLens](https://industry-lens.com/reports/signal-spotlight-knowatoa)). Apart from Peec, traction claims are user counts, which are not comparable to paying customers.

The single closest competitor to AISEO's keyword report is **LLMrefs**. For $79 it tracks 50 keywords, expands each into about 10 prompts and refreshes them weekly across about 10 engines, under the slogan "track keywords, not prompts". In effect it is the recurring version of AISEO's keyword report ([LLMrefs](https://llmrefs.com/pricing)).

Every prompt-volume figure is modeled, because no AI provider publishes how often prompts are asked. Vendors estimate from licensed conversation panels (Profound, Similarweb, Scrunch), from clickstream data (Semrush), from search keywords or Google's People Also Ask questions (Ahrefs, DataForSEO), or from undisclosed models; AthenaHQ claims "95%+" accuracy against unnamed data ([AthenaHQ](https://athenahq.ai/articles/generative-ai-search-query-volume-estimation-model)). Some avoid prompt-level numbers on purpose. Scrunch reports volume only by topic, because "there isn't yet enough reliable, prompt-level data," and Peec shows a relative 1–5 score ([Scrunch FAQ](https://scrunch.com/faqs/how-does-scrunch-estimate-ai-search-volume-metrics); [Peec](https://peec.ai/pricing)).

Critics are blunt. One agency says prompt volumes "convey a pseudo-accuracy," because they are extrapolated from small panels of desktop browser-extension users ([Jaeckert & O'Daniel](https://www.jaeckert-odaniel.com/en/prompt-search-volume-real-data-or-all-guessed/)). Kevin Indig reports that 65–85% of ChatGPT prompts have no matching keyword in Semrush's database ([Growth Memo #17](https://www.growth-memo.com/p/growth-intelligence-brief-17)). He also reports that 95% of the follow-up searches ChatGPT runs behind the scenes have zero search volume ([Growth Memo #16](https://www.growth-memo.com/p/growth-intelligence-brief-16)). Methods built on keyword data miss both by design. The classic question tools have mostly not moved: AlsoAsked offers only an MCP server, and Keywords Everywhere offers only prompt templates ([AlsoAsked](https://alsoasked.com/pricing); [Keywords Everywhere](https://keywordseverywhere.com/pricing.html)).

The sharper warning is that AISEO's format is public. AISEO began as a ground-up rebuild of RankSEO, a public tutorial app first committed on 2 August 2026, and kept its report design ([AISEO README](../README.md)). RankSEO sends keywords to Bright Data's Perplexity scraper, has Claude Sonnet turn the answers into top domains, source mix, competitors, prompt ideas, keyword clusters and a 0–100 score, and runs visibility scans on ChatGPT and Gemini ([RankSEO](https://github.com/TechWithEmmaYT/AI-SEO-Marketing-SaaS-App)). AISEO still matches it closely, using the same three Bright Data scraper datasets, **the same plan quotas** (Free 2 + 2, Pro 100 + 25, Plus 500 + 100), the same scan formula weighting mentions 60 and citations 40, and the same opportunity-score weights of 25, 20, 20, 20 and 15 points ([RankSEO plans](https://github.com/TechWithEmmaYT/AI-SEO-Marketing-SaaS-App/blob/main/lib/plans.ts); [RankSEO pipeline](https://github.com/TechWithEmmaYT/AI-SEO-Marketing-SaaS-App/blob/main/trigger/keyword-research.ts); [AISEO user journeys](../docs/USER-JOURNEYS.md)). Its code is free for personal use and sold under a commercial license. An earlier version of the same pattern (Next.js, Bright Data's Perplexity scraper and GPT-4o) dates from September 2025 ([Sonny Sangha's repo](https://github.com/sonnysangha/seo-AI-saas-brightdata-clerk-billing-nextjs-15)).

AISEO's internal review found that RankSEO does not build, enforces its paywall only in the browser, and scores Bright Data error rows as if they were answers, so a blocked Gemini scrape shows up as "Hidden, 0%" ([AISEO's RankSEO review](../docs/rankseo-review/ARCHITECTURE-REVIEW.md)). AISEO does better on four counts: it asks Perplexity three prompts per keyword instead of one, turns on ChatGPT's web search so answers carry citations, leaves failed answers out of its scores, and refunds the credit when a report fails. Those are real quality differences, but a buyer comparing feature lists cannot see them. Features and quotas are not a moat, so AISEO's moat has to be the quality of its evidence, trust and distribution.

Bright Data starts each ChatGPT request in a fresh session with no memory, and returns the `model` and `web_search_triggered` fields with each answer ([Bright Data docs](https://docs.brightdata.com/datasets/scrapers/scrapers-library/ai-scrapers)). An AIMultiple benchmark, which Bright Data promotes, found it close to 100% successful and the only provider above 90% on Gemini ([AIMultiple](https://aimultiple.com/es/llm-scrapers)). At these prices, data is a rounding error in AISEO's costs: about **$0.0045 of a $0.06 keyword report and $0.015 of a $0.09 scan**.

Every 2026 study that compared interface answers with API answers found large gaps, worst for ChatGPT. Surfer ran 1,000 prompts both ways and found that ChatGPT's interface cited 12.1 sources per answer against 3.1 through the API, with only 4.8% of cited domains appearing in both (26.7% for Perplexity) ([Surfer](https://surferseo.com/blog/llm-scraped-ai-answers-vs-api-results)). Brandlight's 2,565 matched pairs showed the brands named overlapping by only 11% for ChatGPT, 12% for Gemini and 6% for Perplexity ([Brandlight research](https://research.brandlight.ai/ui-vs-api.html)), and Genezio found that none of 3,856 behind-the-scenes searches ChatGPT ran were shared between the API and chatgpt.com ([Genezio](https://genezio.com/research/zero-query-overlaps/)). Each study was run by a vendor that sells interface-based tracking. Conductor, which uses APIs, argues that scraping breaks whenever an interface changes and "likely" violates platform terms, but offers no data ([Conductor Academy](https://conductor.com/academy/scraping-vs-api)). Taken together, the evidence supports AISEO's choice: scraping measures what users see, while an API measures a different answer. One honest caveat applies. A fresh session with no memory is a baseline without personalization, and one unverified UK study found that a logged-in user's history shifted the brands recommended by 16 points on ChatGPT and 33 on Gemini ([EIN Presswire](https://www.einnews.com/pr_news/944542004/new-study-finds-ai-personalization-can-change-which-brands-chatgpt-and-gemini-recommend)).

Answers also vary from one run to the next. SparkToro and Gumshoe found that ChatGPT and Google's AI returned the same list of brands in fewer than 1 in 100 runs, though leading brands still appeared in 55–77% of answers ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-recommendations-change-with-nearly-every-query-sparktoro/566242)). Researchers at the University of St. Gallen argue that visibility should be measured "as a distribution rather than a single-point outcome" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07585)). ThriveStack calculates that zero mentions in 20 runs is still consistent with a true mention rate near 16%, and that about 60 runs are needed before anyone can claim a brand is invisible ([ThriveStack](https://www.thrivestack.ai/research/ai-visibility-tracking-non-determinism)).

By that standard, AISEO's scan is thin. It asks five different prompts once each, so each platform's mention rate rests on five answers. A single answer moves a platform's score by **12 points**. Zero mentions in five answers is consistent with a true mention rate of about **45%** (my calculation, a one-sided 95% upper bound), so a "Hidden" label from one run cannot tell bad luck from real invisibility. Likewise, the keyword report's three Perplexity answers support examples of which domains get cited, not reliable shares of citations.

The category's critics converge on the same points. Rand Fishkin calls any "ranking position in AI" "full of baloney" and tells buyers to make vendors "show their math" ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-recommendations-change-with-nearly-every-query-sparktoro/566242)). Search Engine Land argues that AI share of voice hides an arbitrary set of prompts behind a single percentage ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/ai-share-of-voice-metrics-that-matter-more-479611)). Outcome claims such as Bluefish's "446% ROI" are self-reported and undefined ([Bluefish](https://www.bluefishai.com/)), and nobody has independently audited any tool's accuracy. Few vendors publish sample sizes or margins of error, which makes statistical honesty a cheap way for a small player to stand out.

## AISEO can win as the evidence-first research desk for small teams

### Five openings the leaders leave uncovered

Five gaps stand out. The first is **the small buyer who wants research**. Profound dropped its cheap plans, and most suites charge $99–$199 and price by tracked prompt per day; below about $95 a month, almost nothing combines prompt research with citation analysis except LLMrefs, and AnswerThePublic's $20 AI view, as described, lists intent, sentiment and brands rather than cited sources. The second is **evidence**. The graders hide the prompts and answers, reviewers complain that Peec's visibility percentages come with no explanation, and almost no vendor publishes sample sizes or margins of error, while AISEO already counts mentions and citations in code and shows each prompt's result with an excerpt ([AISEO user journeys](../docs/USER-JOURNEYS.md)).

The third gap is **Perplexity and smaller markets**, which the big datasets serve thinly. Perplexity is missing from Semrush's main database and is about 6% of Ahrefs' index, and Profound's panel is strongest in the US, the UK and some European markets ([Profound help center](https://help.tryprofound.com/articles/4288109168-prompt-volumes)). Perplexity's shrinking share caps this niche, though, and live trackers already cover 42–123 markets, so AISEO's 20 markets help only where its answers are confirmed to be localized. The fourth is **agency pre-sale audits**. Goodie sells 10 pitch workspaces for $275, Otterly bundles pitch workspaces into its agency plans, Profound reportedly charges per client workspace, and AI Peekaboo unlocks white-label reports at $100 ([Goodie](https://higoodie.com/pricing); [Otterly](https://otterly.ai/pricing); [ud.hk](https://www.ud.hk/en/insight/article/profound-alternatives-hong-kong-enterprises-2026-09-23); [AI Peekaboo](https://aipeekaboo.com/pricing)), and AISEO's one-off reports are exactly what a pitch needs.

The fifth gap is **telling users what to do next**, which answers the most repeated complaint, "monitoring without fixes." The cheap vendors that address it hand over lists of where to act, such as Am I On AI's weekly Reddit threads and articles and Promptmonitor's contact details for cited sites ([Am I On AI](https://www.amionai.com/); [Promptmonitor](https://promptmonitor.io/pricing)). Reddit is among the top three cited domains in the interfaces of ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity ([Brandlight research](https://research.brandlight.ai/ui-vs-api.html)), and AISEO could build such lists from the URLs it already collects.

The threats are just as concrete. Incumbent graders and $50 bundles reduce what buyers will pay for a basic visibility check, clones built from public code can match AISEO's features at lower prices, and Perplexity's falling share weakens the only engine behind the keyword report. AISEO also depends on one data provider, whose Gemini scraper cannot target European countries and whose terms-of-service exposure Conductor asserts but no court has tested ([Bright Data docs](https://docs.brightdata.com/datasets/scrapers/scrapers-library/ai-scrapers); [Conductor Academy](https://conductor.com/academy/scraping-vs-api)). The biggest risk is credibility. If most small brands score zero, a single-run "Hidden" verdict will be both AISEO's most common result and its least defensible. One Search Engine Journal study reportedly found only 18 of 177 brands with any AI mention at all, a figure available to me only in a search summary ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-seo-mentions-study-victorious-spa/575040/)).

### Position AISEO as the research step before monitoring

AISEO should stop presenting itself as a cheaper tracker and position itself as **the research step before monitoring**. Its buyers are founders, solo marketers, freelancers, content teams and small agencies who don't pay for Semrush or Ahrefs. For them, AISEO shows what AI assistants actually say about a topic or brand in their market, which sources they cite, which competitors they name and what to publish next, with every number traceable to the answers behind it.

Each competitor group gets one line of contrast. Trackers re-ask 15–50 prompts every day, while AISEO answers hundreds of different questions so you can find the ones worth tracking. Suites need a $99–$199 subscription and estimate from a precomputed index, while AISEO shows live answers, including the Perplexity coverage Semrush's main database lacks. Graders give you a score, while AISEO shows the questions, the answers, the citations and the next step.

The wording should follow the measurement critiques: never say "rank," always give mention rates with the number of answers behind them, label results as a baseline without personalization, and describe the value as influence in AI answers rather than AI traffic. Pro's existing tagline, "for solo marketers and founders," already fits ([AISEO plans](../src/server/billing/plans.ts)). Plus's tagline, "for agencies and growing teams," does not, because Plus has no agency features.

### Price per report, rebuild the free tier and charge agencies for white-label

Keep the $29 entry price. It is the market's anchor, shared with Otterly Lite and Promptmonitor Starter, and going lower would signal a toy when AISEO's cost per answer already equals competitors' retail prices. The changes belong around it: a free tier that meets the graders with an instant preview needing no signup, a one-time pack for occasional buyers (reviewers call subscriptions steep for a casual check, per [That Marketing Buddy](https://thatmarketingbuddy.com/pricing/peekaboo-ai) and [Layer3 Labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/otterly-ai-pricing)), and an agency plan with the features agencies already pay competitors $189–$500 for. The proposal below uses AISEO's own cost estimates. Actual usage, measured in `report_usage`, should set the final allowances.

### Build credibility first, coverage second, retention third

The build order should fix credibility before adding coverage, and coverage before retention, so that spending goes where the evidence says buyers care.

| Order | Build | Evidence | Extra cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Run each scan prompt three times per engine; report mention rates with ranges; say "not detected in N answers" instead of "Hidden" | Single runs are noise: Evertune's ±44 points, SparkToro's under-1% chance of a repeat list, ThriveStack's 60-run threshold | About $0.03 of data per scan, if code counts across all runs and Claude reads one answer per prompt |
| 1 | Public methodology page; show the engine, model, `web_search_triggered`, market and time for each answer | Fishkin's "show their math"; Bright Data already returns these fields | Engineering only |
| 1 | Let users choose the market for scans | Scans send no country today ([brightdata.ts](../src/server/pipeline/brightdata.ts)); trackers cover 42–123 markets | Close to zero; disclose that Gemini can't target Europe |
| 2 | Add Google AI Mode as a second engine in keyword reports | Google surfaces are about 76% of Ahrefs' index; Gemini's referral share grew fivefold while Perplexity's halved | $0.004–$0.012 of data per report via DataForSEO, plus Claude tokens |
| 2 | Cut Claude's cost per report: a smaller model for extraction and classification, cached instructions, batch processing for scheduled runs | Claude is about 83–92% of the cost of goods | Lowers costs and pays for the items above |
| 3 | Scheduled re-runs with trend lines and email digests, drawn from the existing allowance | Trend lines are trackers' core value; AISEO's cost model assumes only 30% of allowances are used | Capped by the allowance |
| 3 | Agency kit: share links, white-label PDFs, client workspaces, bulk keyword upload with a topic map | Agency plans sell for $189–$500; practitioners measure topics rather than single keywords ([Semrush study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-topic-authority-study/)) | Engineering only |
| 4 | A "where to get cited" list built from cited URLs: Reddit threads, listicles, review sites, directories | "Monitoring without fixes" is the top complaint; Reddit is a top-3 cited domain in all three engines' interfaces | Extends an existing Claude call |
| 4 | Back the 0–25 "demand" part of the opportunity score with data, or rename it | Claude infers demand from three answers; volume data is disputed but buyers expect it | DataForSEO AI volume at $0.0001 per keyword, labeled as directional |
| Later | Import the site owner's Bing Copilot queries and Search Console AI impressions | Free data from the platforms themselves, which practitioners have welcomed ([Search Engine Watch](https://searchenginewatch.com/bing-webmaster-tools-ai-performance/)) | Engineering only |
| Avoid | SSO, crawler-log analytics, AI ads, commerce tracking, agents, a race to 10 engines, a prompt-volume panel | Enterprise territory | n/a |

Two of these items need care. Repeated sampling stays cheap only if code counts mentions and citations across every run while Claude reads one representative answer per prompt, because Claude, not data, drives AISEO's costs. Scheduled re-runs should draw on the existing allowance rather than becoming a separate tracking product. That way they give subscribers trend lines and a reason to come back without pulling AISEO into the price war over daily tracking. AISEO should not build anything priced for the enterprise tier, where a $1.8B leader and three acquirers already compete.

## Conclusion

The scarce resource in this market has changed. Collecting AI answers is no longer the hard part, since a scraper delivers them for about a tenth of a cent each. What is scarce now is conclusions that survive a skeptic. The 2026 measurement work, from SparkToro and St. Gallen to Evertune's own published margins of error, has made statistical honesty something buyers check for. Almost nothing under $100 a month offers it, and it costs AISEO pennies per report to provide. A small company cannot outspend Profound or out-bundle Adobe and HubSpot. It can be the tool whose numbers come with their sample size, whose verdicts admit uncertainty, and whose every claim links to the answer behind it.

AISEO's mismatch is commercial rather than technical: it sells a research product packaged like a tracker. Its most dangerous competitors are not the unicorn or the enterprise specialists. They are the incumbents' free graders, which could start showing the prompts and answers they now hide at any time, and public tutorial versions of the same report design. That makes speed on the credibility work and the agency plan worth more than any new engine. The signal to watch over the next two quarters is whether Semrush, now under Adobe, or HubSpot opens up answer-level evidence in its free tools. If either does, AISEO's defensible ground narrows to agencies, the markets panels cover poorly, and the quality of its analysis.
