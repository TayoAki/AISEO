# Enterprise and venture-backed AI search visibility (GEO/AEO) platforms, as of October 2026

*Researched 13 Oct 2026. "Fetched 13 Oct 2026" means the vendor's live page was read that day; most vendor pages show no date beyond "© 2026". Vendor and competitor claims are labelled as such. Anything dated before 2026 is flagged as possibly outdated.*

## 1. Which enterprise/VC-backed platforms lead the category as of October 2026?

### Takeaway
Profound is the clear leader on capital and disclosed traction: a $96M Series C at a $1B valuation (Feb 2026), then a $180M Series D at $1.8B (15 Sep 2026), more than $335M raised in total, and over 1,000 enterprise brands as customers. Behind it is a second tier of enterprise-focused, venture-backed vendors: Bluefish ($68M raised), Brandlight ($36M), Peec AI (about $29M, more mid-market and self-serve), Evertune (about $20M) and Scrunch (about $19-26M raised, then bought by Sitecore in June 2026 for a reported ~$225M). AthenaHQ (YC-backed, about $2.2-2.7M) and Goodie (no disclosed funding) are much smaller companies that still sell enterprise tiers. Between late 2025 and mid-2026, incumbents started buying into the category: HubSpot agreed to buy XFunnel, Sitecore bought Scrunch, and Adobe launched LLM Optimizer and then bought Semrush.

### Inferences
- The category now has three layers: (a) one heavily funded leader (Profound) moving toward an "AI marketing platform" built on agents, ads and commerce; (b) $20-70M enterprise specialists (Bluefish, Brandlight, Evertune, Peec); (c) small vendors with self-serve and enterprise tiers (AthenaHQ, Goodie, Gauge). Incumbents (HubSpot, Sitecore, Adobe) are buying rather than building, so standalone monitoring is turning into a feature of bigger suites.
- Funding counts differ by source because they count different rounds and dates (e.g. Scrunch at $19M vs $26M; Evertune's Series A at $15M vs $19M). Use company press releases as the anchor.

### Gaps
- I found no audited revenue for any vendor. Profound, Bluefish, Brandlight, Evertune and Scrunch publish no ARR.
- I did not research Conductor's or BrightEdge's AI products as standalone platforms. Nothing in 2026 coverage showed them sold separately from their suites. The only Conductor detail found was Profound's own characterization (see section 4).
- I did not profile Adobe LLM Optimizer, which is incumbent territory.

## 2. Published pricing and plan limits (prompts, refresh, seats, brands, platforms, enterprise, contracts, trials)

### Takeaway
Pricing has split in two. Profound (since mid-September 2026), Bluefish and Brandlight are sales-led for brands, with only a free trial or a demo; Evertune now publishes an $800/mo Pro tier. Scrunch, AthenaHQ and Goodie publish self-serve tiers starting at $250-$300/month, and their Pro/Growth tiers run $417-$999/month. Every vendor has a custom Enterprise tier that carries SSO, API access and multiple regions. No enterprise vendor publishes contract lengths. AISEO's $29/$79 plans sit roughly 4-10x below the cheapest paid tier of any vendor in this set.

### Inferences
- **AISEO price position:** AISEO Pro ($29) and Plus ($79) are far below the lowest published paid tier in this set: Scrunch Starter at $250-300, AthenaHQ Starter and Goodie Core at $295, Gauge at $599, Evertune Pro at $800. The enterprise vendors are not competing for AISEO's buyers on price. Profound left the sub-$400 self-serve brand market in September 2026, which widens the gap.
- **Per-response comparison:** AthenaHQ Starter works out to about $0.082 per AI response ($295 / 3,600). AISEO Pro includes 25 scans × 5 prompts × 2 engines = 250 visibility responses (about $20 at AthenaHQ's rate), plus 100 Perplexity keyword reports, for $29. Per response, AISEO is competitive; the enterprise vendors charge for repeated tracking, more engines and workflow tools.
- **AthenaHQ cadence (my arithmetic):** 3,600 credits buy about 18 runs per month of 50 prompts × 4 engines (200 responses per run). That supports weekly tracking but not daily.
- **Evertune Pro:** "100,000 prompts tracked" probably counts sampled responses rather than unique prompts, given Evertune's 100-runs-per-prompt method (section 4). This is my reading, not stated on the page.
- **Common gating:** SSO, API access, multiple regions or languages, and the full engine list are reserved for Enterprise at every vendor. Prompt counts and engine counts are the main levers that separate one tier from the next.

## 3. Which AI platforms each covers

### Takeaway
Entry tiers usually cover a "core five": ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, Perplexity, and either Gemini or Copilot. Claude, Grok, Meta AI, DeepSeek, Mistral and shopping assistants (Amazon Rufus, Alexa, Walmart's Sparky) mostly sit in higher tiers. Commerce surfaces are the newest area: Profound covers ChatGPT Shopping, Goodie lists Alexa and Sparky, and Bluefish lists Alexa for Shopping and Rufus. AISEO covers ChatGPT and Gemini for scans and Perplexity for keyword reports, which is narrower than every competitor's entry tier.

## 4. How each collects data (APIs vs consumer-UI automation, panels/clickstream, sampling, locations, personas)

### Takeaway
The method is a real point of difference, and disclosure is uneven. Profound claims "front-end browser querying" with daily runs, plus a separate licensed, modeled dataset of real conversations (Prompt Volumes). Evertune runs both direct APIs and live consumer apps and samples each prompt 100 times, backed by a 150M+ conversation panel (EverPanel). AthenaHQ, Goodie and Bluefish do not publicly document their methods; Brandlight says only that it asks "thousands of questions from different viewpoints". Tests published by vendors show API and UI answers diverge a lot, especially on citations.

#### Evidence that the collection method changes results
- **Surfer study** (data collected 4 Aug 2026; 1,000 prompts across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, AI Mode and AI Overviews; 13,779 answers; each prompt run once via UI scraping and once via API):
  - Brand overlap between the two methods (Jaccard) was 15.5-23.8% on raw names, 21.3-31.6% after canonicalizing names.
  - ChatGPT's API named 13.8 brands per answer vs 7.9 in the interface, and its answers were 2.05x as long.
  - Cited sources per answer were 12.1 in the ChatGPT interface vs 3.1 via API.
  - Domain overlap was 4.8% for ChatGPT and 26.7% for Perplexity.
  - Surfer sells a scraping-based tracker, and each prompt was run only once — [Surfer](https://surferseo.com/blog/llm-scraped-ai-answers-vs-api-results)
- A guide by an author who works at Peec AI (dated 2 Jul / 6 Sep 2026) says APIs may hit a different model build, may run without web search and miss interface modules. It recommends multiple runs per prompt, ideally daily, and reporting rates rather than single observations — [metehan.ai](https://metehan.ai/articles/how-ai-visibility-tools-collect-data)
- Conductor, an API-first vendor per Profound, notes that AI platforms often serve older or more limited models to logged-out users — [Conductor Academy](https://conductor.com/academy/scraping-vs-api). seoClarity's one-prompt test found that the ChatGPT UI "includes additional logic and response handling not reflected in the API" — [seoClarity](https://www.seoclarity.net/blog/scraping-vs.-api)

### Inferences
- Running the same prompt many times is now standard at the enterprise end: Evertune runs each prompt 100 times, Scrunch runs daily for 14 days, Profound runs daily. AISEO's scan (5 prompts, run once each on ChatGPT and Gemini) is a snapshot. Evertune's own numbers (one prompt asked once: ±44 points) show why a single-run score can be attacked as noise. AISEO could present scans as a "diagnostic snapshot" or add repeat runs.
- AISEO's CLAUDE.md mentions a Bright Data provider (a fake one in local dev), which suggests AISEO collects at the UI level like Profound's "front-end" approach. The Surfer data (ChatGPT UI vs API share only 4.8% of cited domains) supports UI collection for citation-centric reports such as AISEO's. This should be confirmed internally.
- The leaders' "real prompt volume" datasets (Profound Prompt Volumes, Evertune EverPanel) are licensed panel or clickstream data and modeled estimates, not raw platform data. AISEO's prompt ideas come from what Perplexity answers return, a different source with no volume estimates.

### Gaps
- No vendor states whether its UI sessions are logged in or logged out, how it rotates locations or IPs, or which third-party scraping or data providers it uses.
- Bluefish, AthenaHQ and Goodie methods are undocumented. Profound's panel providers and modeling method are undisclosed.
- Peec AI is reported in secondary coverage to scrape the UI deliberately ("what a logged-out user sees"); I did not verify this against Peec's own documentation.
- No independent (non-vendor) audit of any tool's accuracy was found.

## 8. Strengths and weaknesses as reported by reviewers and users

### Takeaway
Independent reviews are thin. Most "best AEO tools" articles are written by competing vendors, and G2 samples are small for everyone except Profound. Recurring themes:
- **Profound:** depth and breadth, but expensive, with a steep learning curve and enterprise-gated features.
- **Scrunch:** strong coverage and personas, but premium pricing, prompt caps and a gap between seeing problems and acting on them.
- **AthenaHQ:** well liked and easy to use, but credit-based costs are hard to predict.
- **Brandlight:** enterprise-only, opaque pricing.
- **Evertune, Goodie, Bluefish:** too few independent reviews to judge.

### Cited Findings
- **G2 ratings (vendor-reported in a Scrunch FAQ, undated):** Scrunch 4.6/5 (55 reviews), AthenaHQ 4.9/5 (27), Peec AI 5/5 (2), Profound 4.6/5 (180) — [Scrunch FAQ](https://scrunch.com/faqs/which-aeo-platforms-have-the-most-public-case-studies-showing-measurable-impact/). Other 2026 counts: Scrunch 4.6 with 73 reviews — [G2 Scrunch](https://www.g2.com/products/scrunch-2026-02-06/reviews); AthenaHQ 4.9 with about 32 reviews — [G2 AthenaHQ](https://g2.com/products/athenahq/reviews). A G2 Profound listing page was titled "Profound Reviews (1,126)" in an Oct 2026 search result — [G2 Profound](https://g2.com/products/profound/reviews_and_filters?page=2)
- **Profound, user complaints on G2:** one reviewer calls it among the most expensive tools; G2's summary says the learning curve "can be steep"; dashboards are "data-heavy"; API access is limited outside Enterprise. Praise centres on interface, support and actionable recommendations — [G2 Profound reviews](https://g2.com/products/profound/reviews?page=5)
- **Profound, reviewer verdict (competitor-authored):** strengths are enterprise logos, SOC 2 Type II and analytics depth; weaknesses are a steeper learning curve, "premium pricing", "limited mid-market fit" and opaque enterprise costs — [Rankability, 2026](https://www.rankability.com/blog/profound-ai-review/)
- **Profound, investment caveat:** with revenue undisclosed, it is hard to tell whether the valuation reflects a growing business or a bet on a category still taking shape. The business also depends on AI models continuing to mention brands in ways that can be measured and influenced — [The Next Web, 15 Sep 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/profound-180m-series-d-ai-answer-engine-optimisation)
- **Scrunch, cons (competitor-authored reviews):**
  - "most premium of its 5 direct competitors"
  - engine coverage and higher features (SSO, API, AXP) gated behind sales or Enterprise
  - prompt caps
  - AXP "still maturing", and "parallel content carries SEO risk"
  - it sells the "see" half well, but the "do something" half is "where most teams quietly hit a wall" — [geotoolbox.ai](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/scrunch-ai-review); [FixAEO](https://fixaeo.com/blogs/scrunch-ai-review/)
- **AthenaHQ:** G2 reviewers (4.9/5 across about 32 reviews, mostly small businesses) praise actionable insights, ease of use, responsive support and frequent updates, and mention a short learning curve — [G2 AthenaHQ](https://g2.com/products/athenahq/reviews). A comparison site flags opaque enterprise pricing and cost unpredictability from the credit model — [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/tools/aeo-ai-visibility/best-athenahq-alternatives)

Brandlight also runs a large set of Q&A comparison pages on sat.brandlight.ai, which rank highly for competitor-pricing queries (seen in search results) — [sat.brandlight.ai](https://sat.brandlight.ai/articles/how-much-more-does-brandlight-cost-than-scrunch-ai)

### Inferences
- **Most reviews are vendor-written:** nearly all the comparison and review content I found was published by competing vendors. Examples: the Scrunch FAQ, Rankability, geotoolbox, FixAEO, LLM Pulse, Conductor's compare pages, and Brandlight's sat.brandlight.ai. Treat every "pros/cons" above as directional.
- **Complaints AISEO answers by design:** price, learning curve, and needing staff to get value from dense dashboards. AISEO's report-style output analyzed by Claude, with low fixed prices, speaks to these. The "see vs do" complaint about Scrunch suggests buyers value next-step recommendations, which AISEO's prompt ideas and keyword clusters partly provide.
- **Methodology as a credibility lever:** AthenaHQ's reported methodology transparency and Evertune's published margins of error show this matters to buyers. A short, public methodology note (which engines, UI vs API, single vs repeated runs) could be a cheap credibility signal for AISEO.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) head-to-head accuracy test of these platforms was found.
- G2 review counts and ratings conflict across sources and dates (e.g. Profound with 180 vs about 1,126 reviews).
- Evertune, Goodie and Bluefish have almost no user reviews visible in public sources.
